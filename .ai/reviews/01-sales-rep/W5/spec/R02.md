# Wave 5 specification review — R02

Date: 2026-09-20  
Scope: `context/features/01-salesrep/wave-5-proposal.md` draft v2 and `R01-response.md`, checked against the
authoritative context, the shipped Wave 3 / 3a code and schema, the Wave 4 dependency boundary, and the planned
production conversion in `context/domain/db-changes.md` §3.3.  
Review type: specification review. Read-only apart from this review record. No database command, schema command,
application-code change, commit, push, or deploy was performed.

## Verdict

**Changes requested. Do not implement the current draft yet.**

The redesign after R01 is materially better. The important product boundaries now make sense:

- one call has one outcome (`INTERESTED`), while the requested contents are multi-valued;
- a client request is not a manager task and is not proof that anything was sent;
- an exact price received by phone or email is the same client-knowledge fact;
- the editable current price remains separate from every amount the client actually received;
- `NOTHING_SPECIFIC` is gone;
- the visible combined send headline is derived rather than encoded in combinatorial enum values;
- migrated old receipts become explicit request rows under Michal's rule instead of creating a permanent legacy mode.

The previous thirteen findings are therefore conceptually answered. However, the new event-ledger design exposes four
implementation blockers that R01 could not assess: chronological matching of requests to backdated/corrected sends,
preservation of a deliberately chosen send step, missing migration provenance, and the undefined non-kind fields of the
derived next step. These are specification gaps, not reasons to abandon `LeadRequest`.

Findings 5–7 are important state/projection/schema gaps. Finding 8 is a small but real authorization ambiguity. The
remaining open product questions Q2 and Q7 are also still a stated freeze gate; they are not counted as defects below.

## Verification of the R01 response

The following R01 changes check out and should be retained:

- PRICE received by phone is a valid receipt. Written confirmation is optional, not a second mandatory content.
- `NOTHING_SPECIFIC` was correctly removed; “Pošlite niečo” maps to Info / ukážky.
- `Lead.askedFor[]` was replaced by event rows, so a later request is not erased by an old receipt.
- `INTERESTED` cleanly separates first-call routing from the requested-content vocabulary. Old `WANTS_*` values remain
  readable and additive rollout is possible.
- The first-call idempotency fingerprint explicitly includes the canonical sorted ask set.
- Wave 5 remains independent from Wave 4 task parts, while still allowing the current one-content task to contribute to
  the checklist.
- The old-send conversion remains mandatory and separate from the request backfill.

Two R01 answers are only partially resolved:

- “Satisfaction is by time” is stated, but the proposed write and correction rules do not actually preserve that
  ordering (Finding 1).
- “A manual plan is preserved” currently protects only CALL / WAIT, not a manually chosen SEND step such as the
  optional written confirmation after a phone price (Finding 2).

## Findings

### 1. BLOCKER — chronological satisfaction and correction cannot be implemented by “close/reopen matching OPEN rows”

**What's wrong**

The proposal correctly says that an old receipt must not satisfy a later request. It then gives commands a different,
state-only rule: a send closes every matching OPEN row, and crossing out that send simply reopens the rows linked to it.
Those rules fail as soon as a send is recorded retrospectively or more than one valid send exists.

The current receipt model makes this distinction unavoidable. `OFFER_SENT.createdAt` is the time the CRM row was
recorded, while `meta.sentOn` is the business day on which the client actually received it. `offerInstant()` deliberately
uses the historical business day for a backdated send. A Wave 5 resolver cannot use insertion order or `createdAt`
alone.

The proposed `requestedAt` is also described as “business day of the call”, although it is a `DateTime` and the design
claims exact event ordering. Same-day live request + phone price, a same-day backdated entry, and migrated synthetic
request + receipt need an explicit tie rule.

**Where**

- `context/features/01-salesrep/wave-5-proposal.md:189-193` — receipt must happen after the request, but a send is also
  said to satisfy all open matching rows.
- `context/features/01-salesrep/wave-5-proposal.md:219-229` — `requestedAt` is a `DateTime` described only as the
  business day of the call.
- `context/features/01-salesrep/wave-5-proposal.md:241-247,267-270` — direct close/reopen operations, with no replay or
  alternate-receipt rule.
- `lib/domain/offers.ts:93-105` — canonical receipt ordering already uses `sentOn`, historical status, and `createdAt`.
- `lib/domain/offerMutations.ts:211-229` — today's correction recomputes canonical receipt summaries; Wave 5 must add
  an equally order-independent request reconciliation rather than a local toggle.

**Concrete failure scenarios**

1. The client asks for PRICE in June and again in September. Both rows are OPEN. In October the manager records a
   forgotten July price email using the historical date. The draft's “close every matching OPEN row” marks both June
   and September satisfied, even though the July email cannot satisfy the September request.
2. The client asks for PRICE, receives price email A, then receives price email B. The request is linked to A. Someone
   crosses out A as a duplicate/wrong record. The draft reopens the request even though valid email B still proves that
   the client received a price after asking.
3. During one follow-up call, the client asks for an exact price and the SR tells it immediately. The request activity
   and phone `OFFER_SENT` are created in one transaction and may have the same database timestamp. The specification
   does not say whether equality satisfies, nor which event wins if code relies on timestamps.

**Why it matters**

This ledger exists specifically so “asked again later” is truthful. A local state flip can create false outstanding
warnings or, worse, hide real work. Corrections would become order-dependent, repeating the class of bug Wave 3a
avoided by recomputing summaries from canonical history.

**Suggested fix**

Define one canonical server-side reconciliation rule and use it for live sends, phone prices, backdated sends,
corrections, and migration:

1. Define the effective request instant and effective receipt instant. A live request should use the exact source
   activity instant, not a rounded business-day start. A receipt uses the existing `offerInstant(meta, createdAt)`
   ordering. A historical day is earlier than a live event recorded later that same day unless an explicit migration
   mapping links them.
2. For each content, replay requests and all **valid** receipts in deterministic business order. A receipt resolves all
   still-open requests whose effective request instant is not later than that receipt. It never resolves a later row.
3. A same-transaction live phone disclosure may explicitly resolve the requests created by that call; do not depend on
   equal timestamps to infer the order.
4. A migrated synthetic `SENT` request is explicitly linked to its source receipt and is valid even when the synthetic
   request and receipt share the same historical day.
5. Crossing out a send runs the same reconciliation against all remaining valid receipts. It reopens a request only
   when no later eligible receipt still satisfies it; otherwise `resolvedActivityId` moves to the valid replacement.
6. Add tests for: a backdated send between two requests, two sends then correction of the first/second, same-day live
   ask + phone price, and a migrated request linked to its receipt.

### 2. BLOCKER — the automatic step policy can erase an explicitly chosen SEND step

**What's wrong**

The product rule says that a phone price satisfies PRICE, but the SR may still deliberately keep “Poslať cenu” to send
written confirmation. The transition policy says automatic recomputation may rewrite any current system send step and
only promises to preserve CALL / WAIT. The stored `Lead.nextActionKind` contains no information saying whether
`SEND_QUOTE` was derived automatically or explicitly chosen by the user.

This is not limited to PRICE. A rep may deliberately choose SEND_EMAIL for an extra unasked email. Once the last open
request is satisfied, a projection based only on open requests has no row from which to render or preserve that plan.

**Where**

- `context/features/01-salesrep/wave-5-proposal.md:49-50,105` — a phone price satisfies the request, but the SR may keep
  SEND_QUOTE manually.
- `context/features/01-salesrep/wave-5-proposal.md:147-157,267-275` — stored kind is recomputed from requests/task state;
  only a manual CALL / WAIT is explicitly protected.
- `lib/commands/dealWork.ts:276-315` — today's follow-up writes the selected next step and then records the phone price
  in the same transaction. Wave 5 must define which operation has precedence.
- `context/app-workflow.md:150` — the current phone-price flow deliberately defaults to a written confirmation step.

**Concrete failure scenario**

The client asks for PRICE during a call. The SR says €1,200 on the phone and keeps “Poslať cenu” because she promised
to email the breakdown. The phone receipt closes the new PRICE request. A generic “recompute from open requests” now
sees no PRICE work and clears/replaces SEND_QUOTE. The deal can move to a follow-up call even though the promised email
was not sent. Conversely, blindly preserving every SEND_QUOTE forever prevents the automatic checklist from shrinking.

**Why it matters**

This can silently remove a real promise to the client. It also means two reasonable implementations of the same draft
will produce different steps depending on whether they process the selected step before or after the phone receipt.

**Suggested fix**

Specify command precedence for **every manually selected next step, including SEND_QUOTE, SEND_DESIGN, SEND_EMAIL and
CUSTOM**, not only CALL / WAIT:

- In a contact transaction, reconcile asks/receipts first and apply an explicitly submitted next step last. The user's
  explicit plan wins.
- Automatic request recomputation may change only a step that is known to be request-derived, or the exact command must
  know that no explicit step was submitted. If that cannot be determined reliably from the command context, add a
  small persisted origin/managed flag; existing steps should migrate as user-owned so rollout never erases them.
- When a manual SEND step has no open request, the visible headline falls back to the explicit step label. It is a
  planned extra/resend, not a false “client still asked and never received” warning.
- Recording the promised send may then complete that manual SEND step normally.
- Test the phone-price + explicit SEND_QUOTE case, an extra unasked SEND_EMAIL, and a later request/correction while
  each manual step is present.

### 3. BLOCKER — migrated requests must be identifiable, but the proposed schema has no provenance or repeatable identity

**What's wrong**

The rollout section requires every backfilled request row to carry migrated provenance and excludes migrated rows from
demand statistics. `LeadRequest` has no `migrated`, origin, provenance, or deterministic migration-key field. An OPEN
row inferred from an old SEND step has no `resolvedActivityId` from which migration status could be derived. A field-only
old receipt may also have no trustworthy original activity/user to place in the required `requestedById`.

The schema also has no uniqueness that makes a rerun of the request backfill safe. `sourceActivityId` is nullable and
not unique with content. A partially failed/retried script can append a second request for the same old receipt or old
step, changing warnings and statistics.

**Where**

- `context/features/01-salesrep/wave-5-proposal.md:213-237` — proposed schema has no migration provenance field or
  deterministic migration identity.
- `context/features/01-salesrep/wave-5-proposal.md:307-308` — demand statistics must exclude migrated rows.
- `context/features/01-salesrep/wave-5-proposal.md:393-415` — backfill creates both SENT and OPEN rows and promises
  `meta.migrated`-style provenance.
- `context/domain/db-changes.md:257-265,294-315` — the canonical old-send conversion requires source IDs,
  confidence/decision provenance, unknown-actor handling, idempotent reruns and full reconciliation.

**Concrete failure scenario**

The production rehearsal creates 2,000 request rows and stops halfway through a later step. It is rerun. Without a
stable key, 2,000 duplicates can be inserted. Alternatively, the script avoids duplicates with an ad-hoc query, but an
old open SEND_EMAIL row has neither a receipt activity nor a source call and cannot be distinguished later from a live
manually added request. Demand statistics count it even though the proposal says migrated rows are excluded.

**Why it matters**

This is both a rollout-safety issue and a permanent statistics-integrity issue. The production database has thousands
of old contacts; “we will know from context” is not durable provenance.

**Suggested fix**

Add an explicit, minimal migration contract to `LeadRequest`, for example:

- an origin such as `LIVE | MIGRATED_RECEIPT | MIGRATED_OPEN_STEP` (or an equivalent migrated flag);
- structured provenance for source field/activity IDs, confidence and the approved exception decision;
- a deterministic nullable `migrationKey` with a unique constraint, or an equally strong unique source identity;
- an explicit rule for inferred rows with no original actor: nullable recorder, or a clearly labelled migration actor
  plus provenance. Do not silently attribute the client's historical request to the current deal owner.

The backfill must be dry-run by default, repeatable, and verify one row per approved source/content identity. Add a
second-run test and an interrupted-rerun test. Define the exact order: canonical old receipt conversion first, request
backfill from those reconciled receipts second, verification third, old-column contraction only later.

### 4. BLOCKER — deriving only `nextActionKind` leaves the scheduling state undefined

**What's wrong**

The proposal defines the dominant kind and the combined visible label, but a next step is five fields:
`nextActionKind`, `nextActionAt`, `nextActionHasTime`, `nextActionMode`, and `nextActionNote`. The date and mode decide
whether the deal appears in Na dnes, Rozpracované or Plánované. No rule says what happens to them when an ask is added,
partially satisfied, corrected, withdrawn, or becomes dominant after another content is sent.

This is especially visible when DESIGN (`IN_PROGRESS`) gives way to PRICE (`SCHEDULED`) or the reverse. Reusing the old
mode/date can hide the deal in the wrong section even when the displayed headline is correct.

**Where**

- `context/features/01-salesrep/wave-5-proposal.md:147-150,192-193,261-275` — specifies the dominant kind and rewrite
  eligibility, but not the other next-action fields.
- `prisma/schema.prisma:93-98` — the persisted step is five coordinated fields.
- `lib/domain/clientSections.ts:54-84` — `nextActionMode` and `nextActionAt` determine the pipeline section.
- `lib/queries/pipeline/index.ts:110-149` — list ordering and TODAY_SQL use the same omitted fields.

**Concrete failure scenario**

The client asks for DESIGN + PRICE. The deal is `SEND_DESIGN / IN_PROGRESS`, starting today. The návrh is sent first,
so PRICE becomes the dominant remaining content. If only the kind changes, the deal becomes `SEND_QUOTE / IN_PROGRESS`
and stays under Rozpracované instead of being due to send today. In the reverse direction, a later DESIGN ask could
inherit a future scheduled price date rather than start its in-progress age now.

**Why it matters**

The CRM's main job is putting work in the correct list. A correct label on a deal that is sorted into the wrong section
is still a workflow failure, and it can break TS ↔ SQL parity assumptions.

**Suggested fix**

Add a normative transition table for the full step state:

- first/open dominant DESIGN: kind SEND_DESIGN, mode IN_PROGRESS, start date policy defined;
- first/open dominant PRICE: kind SEND_QUOTE, mode SCHEDULED, due-date policy defined;
- INFO / PRICELIST / REVIEW only: SEND_EMAIL, mode/date policy defined;
- dominance changes after partial send/withdraw/correction;
- task creation/lock and task close/unlock;
- no outstanding content;
- explicit manual step (Finding 2).

Define whether the relevant request date or “today” becomes `nextActionAt`, how the note is preserved/replaced, and
what the visible combined headline uses instead of `NEXT_ACTION_LABEL`. Extend the existing clientSection/TODAY_SQL and
ordering parity tests to every transition, not only the resulting enum kind.

### 5. IMPORTANT — closing and reopening a deal with OPEN requests has no defined lifecycle

**What's wrong**

The test list mentions closed/reopened deals, but no operation says what happens to OPEN request rows on WON, LOST,
UNREACHABLE or later reopen. Current close commands clear the step; current manager reopen creates a fixed CALL step.
The request ledger would remain OPEN unless Wave 5 adds another rule.

**Where**

- `context/features/01-salesrep/wave-5-proposal.md:239-248,261-275` — request states and operations contain no close or
  reopen rule.
- `context/features/01-salesrep/wave-5-proposal.md:377` — asks for a closed/reopened test without defining the expected
  result.
- `lib/commands/dealWork.ts:288-302` — current close clears the next step.
- `context/features/01-salesrep/wave-3-task-proposal-final.md:360` — current reopen intentionally sets CALL today.

**Concrete failure scenario**

A client asked for a návrh, then says they are no longer interested before it is sent. The SR closes the deal LOST.
Two weeks later the manager reopens it because the closure was mistaken. Depending on the implementation, the old
DESIGN request may be silently withdrawn, may instantly replace the fixed reopen CALL with SEND_DESIGN, or may stay
OPEN but invisible. All three are plausible from the draft.

**Why it matters**

The choice affects historical truth, reopened workflow and warning counts. Implementers should not invent it while
writing a status command.

**Suggested fix**

Choose one explicit policy. The least destructive/simple policy is:

- closing does not rewrite historical client requests; OPEN rows become dormant because outstanding projections and
  warnings apply only to open deals;
- reopening keeps the existing fixed CALL-today plan as an explicit manual step and immediately shows the outstanding
  warning/checklist;
- the manager then sends it or withdraws it with a reason if it is obsolete.

If Michal instead wants closure to withdraw pending requests, specify a system withdrawal reason and whether reopen
can restore them. Add close/reopen/correction tests for the chosen policy.

### 6. IMPORTANT — raw request events and the actionable checklist need different cardinality

**What's wrong**

The ledger deliberately allows repeated requests. The checklist/headline is described as the OPEN rows, but the draft
does not say that actionable contents are grouped by content. Two PRICE requests before the next send could therefore
render two Cena rows, “Poslať cenu + cenu”, two pre-ticked checkboxes, or inconsistent counts between the detail and
list. Demand statistics, on the other hand, may legitimately count both request events.

**Where**

- `context/features/01-salesrep/wave-5-proposal.md:118-120` — every later tick creates a new request.
- `context/features/01-salesrep/wave-5-proposal.md:144-149` — checklist/headline are derived from outstanding rows.
- `context/features/01-salesrep/wave-5-proposal.md:191,239-247` — one send closes multiple rows, while the UI projection
  is not given a grouping rule.
- `context/features/01-salesrep/wave-5-proposal.md:296-308` — demand statistics use the raw request rows.

**Concrete failure scenario**

The client asks for a price on Monday and repeats the request on Wednesday before the manager provides it. There are two
valid OPEN PRICE events. Operationally there is one thing to send and one checkbox. Analytically there were two asks.
Without a canonical grouped projection, each screen can answer differently.

**Why it matters**

Duplicate actions make the UI look broken and can cause a partial send to appear incomplete even though the server
closed both rows.

**Suggested fix**

Define one pure `clientRequestProjection` (name illustrative):

- history/“Chceli” may show each event with its date;
- actionable outstanding contents are a unique set grouped by `RequestContent`;
- checklist, headline, warnings, manager preselection and send-dialog defaults all consume that grouped projection;
- one eligible receipt resolves all eligible open events of that content;
- demand statistics consume raw LIVE events, not the grouped obligations and not migrated rows.

Add a parity test with two OPEN requests of the same content and one send.

### 7. IMPORTANT — the proposed Prisma model does not declare the relations that its invariants rely on

**What's wrong**

The schema sketch declares `lead` and `requestedBy` relation fields but omits their opposite fields on `Lead` and
`User`; as shown, it is not a complete valid Prisma schema. More importantly, `sourceActivityId`, `resolvedActivityId`
and `resolvedById` are unvalidated scalar strings even though the proposal calls those links the exact audit proof of
where a request came from and which receipt resolved it.

A normal foreign key still cannot prove that a linked Activity belongs to the same Lead, so the command must validate
that under the Lead lock as well.

**Where**

- `context/features/01-salesrep/wave-5-proposal.md:213-233` — partial relation model and scalar audit links.
- `prisma/schema.prisma:10-53,78-165,177-204` — current opposite relation collections that must be extended.

**Concrete failure scenario**

An implementation typo resolves a request on lead A with an OFFER_SENT activity from lead B. With plain string IDs the
database accepts it. The checklist for A closes, corrections on B may reopen A's row, and neither relation query nor
foreign-key failure exposes the mistake.

**Why it matters**

`resolvedActivityId` is the proposed source of truth for correction and history. A dangling or cross-lead ID corrupts
that truth silently.

**Suggested fix**

Make the schema proposal complete before implementation:

- add the `Lead` and `User` back-relations;
- add explicit optional relations for source activity, resolving activity and resolver user (with intentional delete
  behaviour);
- decide whether a unique source/content constraint belongs in live rows as well as migration rows;
- under the Lead lock, assert that every linked Activity has the same `leadId`, expected type and valid state/channel;
- test cross-lead IDs, reverted receipt IDs and deactivated users.

### 8. MINOR — `setClientAsksAs` authorization wording contradicts the decided manager rule

**What's wrong**

The operation table says “Owner or manager on an ownerless deal”, which can be read as allowing a manager only when the
deal has no owner. Q5 says the manager may edit “Chceli” on a rep's deal. The current permission architecture also
expects a manager to work across all scoped deals.

**Where**

- `context/features/01-salesrep/wave-5-proposal.md:268`
- `context/features/01-salesrep/wave-5-proposal.md:359`
- `context/project-overview.md:46-58` — owner work versus manager-wide deal permissions.

**Concrete failure scenario**

An implementer follows the operation table literally and rejects Michal's edit on an SR-owned deal. Another follows
Q5 and permits it. Both can claim compliance with the same proposal.

**Why it matters**

It is a small sentence, but it sits directly beside the command contract and can produce the wrong access guard.

**Suggested fix**

Write the rule unambiguously: **the current deal owner may edit; a manager with `deals.manage` may edit any in-scope
deal, including another rep's deal; an ownerless deal is manager-only; every out-of-scope access returns NOT_FOUND.**

## Accepted design choices — do not reopen without new evidence

- A phone PRICE receipt satisfies a PRICE request. Written confirmation is optional and represented by the explicit
  next step, not a second receipt state.
- `INTERESTED` is the right additive first-call outcome. Do not turn `CallOutcome` into an array and do not map a
  multi-content call back to one dominant `WANTS_*` value.
- `NOTHING_SPECIFIC` should remain removed.
- Repeated requests being event rows is not excessive form-filling: users still tick once; the complexity stays in the
  server so a later ask cannot be hidden by an old send.
- One receipt may satisfy several eligible open requests of the same content.
- No combinatorial next-action enums are needed. The visible combined headline is a derived label.
- Manager task contents remain PRICE / DESIGN / OTHER. INFO, PRICELIST and REVIEW never become manager work merely
  because the client asked for them.
- The known Wave 3 limitation while a task remains locked (no separate scheduled call) remains accepted and was not
  reported again.
- Wave 5's schema can remain additive. The old-column removal is a later, separately approved non-additive contraction.

2. Decide Q7: showing the outstanding warning immediately is the simpler rule and matches the proposal's purpose.
3. Incorporate Findings 1–8 and rerun a short specification review.

5. Only then freeze the file as `wave-5-proposal-final.md` and apply S-16–S-19 on the test branch using the documented

## Final recommendation

**Accept the architecture, revise the state machine and migration contract.** No redesign back to a lifetime array or
to combined enums is warranted. Once the proposal defines chronological reconciliation, manual-step precedence, the
full next-action transition, migrated-row identity/provenance, close/reopen behavior and the grouped projection, it is
reasonable to implement on the test branch and then re-review Wave 4 against the shipped operations.
